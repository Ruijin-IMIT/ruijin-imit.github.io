BHANMDingsd

Leader: Shaodong Ding · Beihang University, China
Classification 1st · $1,000 Segmentation 4th · $400
MRN: Multi-ROI driven classification Network

Crops four clinically relevant ROIs from NM-MRI and QSM, encodes axial slices with DINOv2, and fuses volumetric context through Feature Fusion Self-Attention and Slice Fusion Self-Attention. An auxiliary DPT segmentation head steers features toward the target nuclei, and multi-ROI supervised contrastive learning (mSupMoCo) improves class separation.

86.0% test accuracy — 5.5 percentage points ahead of the runner-up.

PolyUAIM

Leader: Yingji Fu · The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, Hong Kong, China
Segmentation 1st · $1,000
Dual-collaborative knowledge distillation

Couples nnU-Net with a SwinUNETR foundation model through bidirectional knowledge distillation, combining local boundary sensitivity with global contextual priors. Cross-model distillation, self-pseudo regularization and feature alignment are dynamically weighted during training, with a structure-aware loss that emphasizes small nuclei.

Best overall segmentation score 9.2469; best SNc Dice on NM-MRI 0.882.

Space

Leader: Chenyuan Bian · The Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University, China
Segmentation 2nd · $600 Classification 3rd · $600
BLU-Net: Bootstrapped Learning Unified Network

A U-shaped encoder–decoder built from hierarchical gated convolution units. The network is pretrained on the MSD brain-tumor segmentation dataset and fine-tuned for multimodal PD segmentation, with the same backbone adapted to classification.

Segmentation 9.1988; classification 79.5%.

NIMG

Leader: Shuxin Cao · Nanjing University of Science and Technology, China
Segmentation 3rd · $600
U-Mamba state-space framework

Uses a U-Mamba architecture that combines state-space modeling with a U-Net design to capture long-range 3D dependencies at linear complexity. T1w, QSM and NM-MRI are concatenated as multi-channel input, with a composite Dice and cross-entropy loss for small-structure imbalance.

Segmentation 9.1246; best Dice for CN (0.898), STN (0.735), RN (0.907) and DN (0.880).

MThirtyOne AI

Leader: Aaron Choi · M31 / UTHealth Houston, Canada / USA
Classification 2nd · $600
VoCo-pretrained 3D residual encoder

A six-stage 3D residual encoder initialized with VoCo volume-contrastive weights, using NM-MRI only after controlled comparisons showed it was the strongest single modality. A two-stage warm-up curriculum freezes the head first and then unfreezes the encoder with differential learning rates.

80.5% test accuracy; VoCo pretraining improved NM-MRI validation accuracy to 81.0%.

PRLABbySMU

Leader: Seung Min Jeong · Sangmyung University, South Korea
Classification 4th · $400
InceptionAPA: 2D–3D hybrid

An InceptionNeXt slice encoder with early multimodal fusion and attention-guided pooling across slices. The lightweight model (about 28 M parameters) is designed as a compute-efficient alternative to direct transfer from large radiology foundation models.

76.5% test accuracy, 12.5 points above the RadCLIP transfer baseline.

ZhangFan_Lab

Leader: Chengjin Li · University of Electronic Science and Technology of China, China
Segmentation 5th · $400 Classification 5th · $400
3D-F3Net: foundation-model feature fusion

Combines frozen DINOv3 semantic features with a parameter-efficiently fine-tuned SAM2 branch. A Gated Attention Fusion Module weights the two feature sources, and a 3D Receptive Field Block captures multi-scale context for both segmentation and classification.

Segmentation 8.6180; classification 75.5%.

NeuroX

Leader: Chenhua Zhang · Fudan University, China
Segmentation 6th · $400 Classification 6th · $400
nnU-Net transfer + TabPFN features

Pretrains nnU-Net on BraTS 2021 and fine-tunes it separately for QSM and NM-MRI, then extracts 468 quantitative features (volume, first-order statistics, GLCM texture, shape and lateralization). A TabPFN tabular foundation model performs interpretable classification.

Segmentation 8.4768; classification 74.0%.

Workshop and technical reports

The winning teams presented their solutions at the PDCADxFoundation workshop at MICCAI 2025 in Daejeon on 27 September 2025. All eight teams submitted technical reports describing their methods; these reports, together with the team rosters collected at check-in, form the basis of the challenge summary paper submitted to Medical Image Analysis (manuscript MEDIA-D-26-04385).
